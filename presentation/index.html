Opening

Generative AI has made it possible for the visible artifact of learning to remain intact even when some of the thinking, articulation, and ownership that artifact once signaled have been outsourced. This proposal asks how educators and school-system leaders can tell the difference between useful support and educational bypass.

[Sources] Miner dissertation proposal working copy, title page and Overview, July 24, 2026.

The opening tension

A finished artifact no longer reliably shows the thinking behind it.

Two submitted artifacts

what an evaluator can see

The route each one took

drafted, reconsidered, revised
produced in one pass

This is an evidence problem before it is a misconduct problem.

Talk track

A completed essay, explanation, or solution has never been perfect evidence of learning. Under generative AI, however, fluent performance can be produced with much less interpretive and authorial work. The proposal centers that broken inference, not academic misconduct alone.

[Sources] Proposal, Chapter One, Background of the Study and Statement of the Problem; Bastani et al. (2025); Kizilcec et al. (2024); Schindler and Richter (2023).

Author-weighted descriptive estimates from RAND/Gallup public-use data — contextual indicators, not proposed-study findings. Weighting and noncomparability cautions: Miner (2026a), doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21152544

Talk track

The proposal uses national data to establish the policy moment, not to measure pedagogical friction. The key contextual pattern is that classroom adoption is expanding much faster than shared guidance, professional learning, and policy clarity.

Use cautious language. These values are descriptive estimates. The proposal notes differences in instruments, samples, definitions, and available survey-design information.

[Sources] Proposal, Chapter Two, K–12 AI Adoption and Institutional Readiness, Table 1; RAND/Gallup files identified in the proposal.

The media-ecological claim

The shift is from algorithmic selection to algorithmic production.

This is not a claim of inevitability. If AI’s effects were determined, pedagogical friction would be pointless.

Primary orality Knowledge lives in speech, memory, and performance. What cannot be remembered cannot persist. Ong (1982/2002)
Literacy Writing externalizes memory. Analysis, revision, and a durable record become possible. Ong (1982/2002)
Secondary orality Electronic media restore oral immediacy at scale, but the expression is still human-produced. Ong (1982/2002)
Algorithmic secondary orality Names the social-media condition in which human content is selected and amplified algorithmically. Researcher’s extension of Ong (1982/2002) and Stalder (2016/2018)
Tertiary algorithmicity Names the generative condition in which production itself also becomes algorithmic at scale. The question is no longer speech versus writing, but whether human expression is required at all. Researcher’s extension of Ong (1982/2002) and Stalder (2016/2018)

Stages 04 and 05 are the researcher’s extensions of Ong (1982/2002) and Stalder (2016/2018). Distinct from existing tertiary-orality scholarship: that work asks what happens to oral expression in digital environments; this asks whether human expression is required at all.

Talk track

Bridge from the previous slide. Adoption and policy describe how fast schools are moving. They do not explain what is actually changing underneath. That requires naming the media condition.

Ong explains how changes in dominant media reshape consciousness and culture. Algorithmic secondary orality names the social-media condition in which human content is selected and amplified algorithmically. Tertiary algorithmicity names the generative condition in which production itself also becomes algorithmic at scale.

Do not present this sequence as inevitable technological progress. It is a periodizing analytic frame with porous boundaries and hybrid practices.

If asked, is this just tertiary orality? A body of work across linguistics, media ecology, library science, and sociology has proposed tertiary orality for digitally mediated expression: Mayer (2009), Turner and Allen (2013), Soffer (2020), Heyd (2021), Ryu (2023), and Cordón-García and Muñoz-Rico (2024). That literature already recognizes interactivity, voice interfaces, and human-machine discourse. What it does not do is make the automated generation of extended symbolic content the organizing principle of its periodization, or develop the consequences for educational evidence of learning, authorship, and accountability. The distinction is not speech versus writing. It is between environments where algorithms mainly circulate human expression and environments where they can also generate the artifact.

If asked about determinism. The framework is a response framework. Naming friction as something educators design asserts agency within a powerful default rather than claiming the default is destiny (Feenberg, 2002; Hutchby, 2001).

[Sources] Proposal, Chapter Two, Media Ecology and Ong's Developmental Account; Guarding Against Technological Determinism; Prior Extensions and the Limits of Tertiary Orality; Algorithmic Secondary Orality and the Transitional Stage; Tertiary Algorithmicity and the Generative Condition; Ong (1982/2002); Stalder (2016/2018).

Noetic displacement and the bypass of cognitive labor

AI can route around the formation the assignment was meant to produce.

Task Encounter a problem
Learner process Interpret · struggle · articulate · revise
Artifact Submit a fluent product
Generative AI
  • Noetic displacement AI substitutes for the cognitive work that builds understanding
  • Rhetorical saturation Generated language weakens exchange and obscures the source of a claim
  • Existential abstraction The artifact separates from anyone willing to own and defend it

Unproductive success: correct-looking performance without the struggle that performance normally indicates.

Talk track

This is not the claim that every use of AI eliminates learning. It names a possibility that educators increasingly need to diagnose. A learner may complete the task and receive credit while the intended noetic, rhetorical, or existential formation happens elsewhere, or not at all.

These three pressures are the hinge of the argument. Each names a condition of Ong's account under strain, and each has a matching form of friction on the next slide. Name them here so the framework reads as a response rather than an assertion.

Unproductive success is adapted from Kapur's fourfold distinction among productive success, productive failure, unproductive failure, and unproductive success. It is the diagnostic term the study asks participants to recognize in practice.

[Sources] Proposal, Chapter Two, Noetic Displacement and the Bypass of Cognitive Labor; Rhetorical Saturation and the Erosion of Genuine Exchange; Existential Abstraction and the Severing of Claim From Claimant; Definitions of Key Terms; Kapur (2016); Miner (2026b).

Answers Noetic displacement AI substitutes for the cognitive work that builds understanding. Sustained or constrained by infrastructural friction.
Answers Rhetorical saturation Generated language weakens exchange and obscures the source of a claim. Sustained or constrained by infrastructural friction.

Each form answers one pressure from the previous slide. The framework is the study’s lens and its object: its usefulness is an empirical question, not a commitment the data must confirm.

Talk track

Noetic, rhetorical, and existential friction are analytically separable but operationally entangled. Infrastructural friction is different in kind. It is not simply a fourth learner experience. It names the institutional conditions that enable or constrain the other three. That relationship, that institutional conditions enable or constrain classroom friction, is the central claim the study examines rather than assumes.

If asked about cognitive augmentation. The strongest objection is that AI extends cognition as prior tools did, and that resisting it repeats the error of condemning writing or the calculator (Mollick, 2024; Riva, 2025). The framework concedes that tools extend cognition and that this has happened before. Its reply is narrower: the question is not whether a tool extends adult expert cognition, but whether routine use during formation prevents novices from building the schemas expertise requires. Calculator research complicates the analogy in the objection's favor, since integrated calculator use has generally not hindered mathematical skill development (Ellington, 2003). Concede that, then restate the narrower question: does the tool extend an already developing schema, or substitute for the practice that builds it?

Committee reference. The framework, instruments, and secondary-data materials are laid out on the proposal's digital companion site: minerclass.github.io/pedagogical-friction/

[Sources] Proposal, Chapter One, Conceptual Framework; Chapter Two, The Pedagogical Friction Framework; Counterarguments and the Limits of the Framework. Original framework by Miner.

The equity boundary on the framework

The question is not whether school should be difficult.

Preserve

Productive friction

Effort that builds understanding, judgment, authorship, and capacity.

Situated professional judgment
Remove

Exclusionary friction

Barriers that block access without serving the intended learning.

The same AI use may change both kinds of friction at once. No policy resolves this in advance, which is why the study asks how educators actually draw the line.

Talk track

This slide is the constraint on everything before it. A defense of friction becomes a defense of inequity if the study does not distinguish learning-producing difficulty from needless barriers. Every dimension on the previous slide is subject to this boundary.

The distinction is situated. The multilingual-learner paradox is the clearest case: a student writing in an additional language who uses AI translation may bypass an exclusionary barrier while also complicating the relationship among language choice, voice, reasoning, and authorship. No mechanical resolution is possible. The analytic question is what work the tool removed and whether that work served the learning goal.

Dolmage (2017) argues that what higher education calls rigor has often functioned as gatekeeping. Annamma et al. (2013) show how race and disability intersect so that what counts as productive struggle for one student is documented as deficit for another. This is why the framework cannot resolve the boundary in advance, and why the dissertation investigates how educators draw it in practice.

[Sources] Proposal, Chapter One, Statement of the Problem and Conceptual Framework; Chapter Two, Productive and Exclusionary Friction and the Equity Boundary; Annamma et al. (2013); Dolmage (2017); Kasneci et al. (2023).

Exact research questions

RQ1. How do classroom-facing educators, building-level administrators, district or system-level leaders, and adult university students make sense of the ways generative AI changes the effort, dialogue, authorship, judgment, and participation involved in teaching and learning?

RQ2. How do educators and school-system leaders make sense of and respond to the policy, professional-learning, assessment, access, and governance conditions surrounding generative AI?

RQ3. What language, assessment expectations, instructional practices, professional supports, and leadership approaches do educators and school-system leaders identify as important for preserving forms of friction that support learning while reducing unnecessary barriers?

[Sources] Proposal, Chapter One, Research Questions.

Talk track

The notation is QUAL + quan. Constructivism governs the research questions and primary qualitative interpretation. Pragmatism provides the narrower warrant for bringing several forms of evidence into conversation. The design seeks understanding, not causal effects or statistical generalization.

Talk track

Purposeful sampling seeks information-rich experience and contextual diversity, not statistical representation. Diversity is pursued across role, content area, school poverty concentration, locale, and district AI-policy maturity. Recruitment also prioritizes educators who support multilingual learners and students with disabilities.

Adult university students are not equated with the professional role groups. They provide a distinct retrospective perspective on learning before and after broad public access to generative AI.

[Sources] Proposal, Chapter Three, Participants and Sampling.

Evidence architecture

Each source answers a different weakness in the evidence.

Primary qualitative evidence

Semistructured interviews · card-sort explanations · institutional documents · open-ended survey responses

Supporting quantitative and structural context

Closed-ended educator survey · NCES School Pulse Panel · RAND educator datasets

Separately labeled nonparticipant comparison

AI-generated texts, agentic artifacts, and process traces

No source validates or overrides another.

Talk track

Interviews provide depth but remain vulnerable to social desirability. Card sorts require judgments about ambiguous cases. Documents offer a non-self-report comparison. The survey broadens participation. National datasets locate the accounts within a larger policy moment. AI materials remain nonparticipant artifacts.

[Sources] Proposal, Chapter Three, Data Sources and Instruments; How the Evidence Sources Work Together; Table 2.

Analysis and integration

Integration preserves difference instead of forcing agreement.

QUAL

quan

Descriptive and exploratory statistics. Below 80 shared-core responses, frequencies only.

Joint displays + narrative weaving
Convergence Expansion Divergence Silence

Talk track

Qualitative themes are developed first through engagement with participant language, context, and disagreement. Quantitative sources are analyzed within their own limits. Joint displays then make the relationship among sources visible. Divergence is retained as a finding rather than treated as error.

If asked what happens if the survey underperforms. The primary statistical record is maintained in R, with selected headline estimates reproduced in jamovi as a verification check. If the achieved shared-core sample falls below 80, analysis is limited to descriptive frequencies, role-based patterns where appropriate, and narrative integration. Where role-based branching reduces usable sample size, the study reports descriptive evidence rather than unstable subgroup claims. Item-total and internal-consistency estimates are reported only as preliminary evidence of item behavior, never as validation of the framework as a scale.

Don't-know responses on infrastructural items are interpreted substantively, not just excluded. A high don't-know rate on policy or guidance items is itself a signal of weak infrastructural clarity.

[Sources] Proposal, Chapter Three, Qualitative Analysis; Quantitative Analysis; Mixed Methods Integration; Braun and Clarke (2006, 2021); Fetters et al. (2013).

Rigor, power, and ethics

The study makes the researcher’s investment visible and challengeable.

“The framework’s utility is treated as an empirical question, not a commitment the data must confirm.”
  • Inductive questions before framework-laden prompts
  • Reflexive and analytic memoing across the full study
  • Negative-case analysis and explicit invitations to disagree
  • Member checking for accuracy and interpretive clarification
  • Sole analyst by design; peer debriefing on ≈20% of transcripts for challenge, not intercoder agreement
  • IRB approval, consent, de-identification, and secure storage

Talk track

My professional role and public technoskeptical position are sources of insight and bias. The design does not claim neutrality. It makes interpretation traceable through memos, an audit trail, disconfirming evidence, and structured challenge. Primary data collection occurs outside my own district.

[Sources] Proposal, Chapter Three, Researcher Positionality, Power, and Ethics; Researcher-Controlled Analytic Workflow; Trustworthiness, Validity, and Reliability.

Agentic AI boundary

A system response is not evidence of agentic execution.

Any agentic claim requires a prespecified, bounded multistep task with enabled and captured planning, tool use, execution, and process traces.

  • Comparison begins only after themes from human accounts are finalized
  • No participant data supplied to any system
  • No actions that modify external data, systems, accounts, or affect people
  • Tools, permissions, memory, network access, approvals, stopping conditions, failures, and outputs documented
  • Materials interpreted only as system-, configuration-, task-, and time-specific nonparticipant artifacts

The result is an exploratory capability snapshot, not evidence of classroom deployment.

Talk track

Generative AI remains the empirical focus. The agentic component is deliberately narrow because agentic systems are rapidly changing and are not being studied as classroom interventions. The component examines the extension from output generation toward multistep execution while retaining strict evidence and safety boundaries.

[Sources] Proposal, Chapter One, Background of the Study; Chapter Three, AI-Generated Text and Agentic Artifact Comparison; Limitations.

What the study can contribute

The framework earns value only if it helps interpret practice and survives contradiction.

Scholarly

Examines whether a media-ecological and learning-science framework helps explain K–12 sensemaking.

Practical

Offers language for distinguishing support, augmentation, bypass, and exclusionary barriers.

Policy

Connects AI governance to the conditions of learning, not compliance alone.

Evidence that does not fit will revise, qualify, or extend the framework.

Talk track

The proposal does not treat tertiary algorithmicity or pedagogical friction as findings. It asks whether those concepts help participants name and interpret what they encounter. The design is successful if it produces a more defensible account, including evidence that shows where the framework fails.

Key limitations include self-report, retrospective student accounts, nonprobability survey recruitment, national data that do not directly measure pedagogical friction, and rapidly changing AI systems.

[Sources] Proposal, Chapter One, Significance of the Study; Chapter Three, Limitations.

Closing

State the ask plainly. The committee is not being asked to accept tertiary algorithmicity or pedagogical friction as true. It is being asked whether the research questions, sampling, evidence boundaries, analysis, and safeguards can examine the framework credibly, including the possibility that the evidence revises, qualifies, or extends it.

The study keeps human sensemaking primary, keeps equity inside the central distinction rather than beside it, and preserves the possibility that the framework must change.
